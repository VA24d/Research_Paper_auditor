Real papers with known errors

Our test sets use planted errors. These papers had real ones, reported in the news, in retraction notices or in the SPOT benchmark’s annotations, and the auditor found them.

In the news · NeurIPS 2025
Fabricated references in accepted NeurIPS papers
In January 2026 GPTZero listed 100 fabricated citations in 51 accepted NeurIPS 2025 papers (The Register). ACL 2026 later desk-rejected more than 100 accepted papers for the same reason.

We ran the citation checker on two of the listed papers, using the arXiv versions from before their authors fixed the references. It flagged all three references GPTZero named.

arXiv:2510.21310v1 · semantic uncertainty

“Nuo Lou and et al. Dsp: Diffusion-based span prediction for masked text modeling. arXiv preprint arXiv:2305.XXXX, 2023.”

“… Efficient few-shot learning with efl: A single transformer for all tasks. In arXiv preprint arXiv:2107.13586, 2021.”

Auditor: no work with either title in the indexes it searched (OpenAlex, Crossref, arXiv, Semantic Scholar). Both went to a person for review.

arXiv:2506.12241v2 · privacy reasoning

“Zayne Sprague, Xi Ye, Kyle Richardson, and Greg Durrett. MuSR: Testing the limits of chain-of-thought with multistep soft reasoning. In EMNLP, 2023.”

Auditor: the paper exists, but Kyle Richardson is listed as an author. The real paper has five authors, and he is not one of them. This was a verified flag.

Each paper also got other reference flags for a person to check (38 and 22). Some come from lookups that the Semantic Scholar rate limit cut short.

Retracted preprint · SPOT benchmark
Every Polish group has a non-trivial topological group automorphism
arXiv:2408.16162v2, retracted. One of the papers in SPOT's math set.

What the paper says (proof of Theorem 1.1)

“we take any non-trivial automorphism φ of span{xi}i<n and extend it to a ℤ2-automorphism of H by setting φ(y) = y for y ∈ Y.”

What the auditor reported

Take G = (ℤ2)ω and L = span{x1, x2}. If y1 + y2 + y3 = x1 for some yj ∈ Y, then any map that fixes Y also fixes x1, so an automorphism of L that moves x1 cannot be extended this way.

Found. SPOT's annotation gives the same cause: fixing Y can redefine the automorphism on elements of L that Y generates. The judge model matched our report to it. The run marked it for a person to review rather than reporting it outright.

Retracted · arXiv:2403.01085v2
A strongly subcubic combinatorial algorithm for triangle detection

Paper: if one triangle vertex a is on BFS level 1, then b and c are on level 2 and the triangle is found while scanning b.

Auditor: a counterexample. Let the start vertex also reach b through another vertex u processed before a. Then b’s parent is u, and scanning b does not reveal the triangle. This is the flaw in Case 2.1 that led to the retraction.

Retracted · arXiv:2407.18406v2
A refined Roth’s theorem and its application to the abc conjecture

Paper: by Dirichlet’s unit theorem there is a constant c(κ) with |y|v ≤ c(κ)|y|w for any Archimedean v, w.

Auditor: in ℚ(√2), yn = (1+√2)n makes the ratio of the two absolute values (1+√2)2n, which is unbounded, so no such constant exists. The step that would have implied the abc conjecture fails.

Kept after a correction · arXiv:2312.11580v1
PlaNet-S: placenta segmentation

Paper, in the abstract and twice in the results: “PlaNet-S had significantly higher IoU (0.73 ± 0.13) than that of U-Net (0.78 ± 0.01).”

Auditor: the text says PlaNet-S is higher, and the numbers say the opposite. The authors’ v2 corrected the standard deviation but kept this sentence.

For the two retracted papers, both judge models matched the auditor’s report to the annotated error. All four findings were marked for a person to review rather than reported outright.

Abstract

Reviewers rarely open the papers a submission cites or the code it releases, so a wrong citation or a setting that differs from the code usually goes unnoticed. We built an auditor that checks a paper (PDF or LaTeX) against the papers it cites and its code repository. It splits the paper into small items and sends each item to a specialist checker. It reports a problem only when it can quote the evidence and a verifier finds that quote in the source.

We compared it with two baselines on the same model. B0 (LLM) reads the paper once with no tools. B1 (Agent) is a single agent with the same tools but no verifier. The test set has 40 papers with their code. Each paper appears twice, as the original and as a copy with planted errors (80 paper versions, 163 labelled errors, 72 unchanged control items). Over three runs the full system scores a Macro-F1 of 0.636, against 0.048 for B0 (LLM) and 0.046 for B1 (Agent). No system flags any control item. The full system also costs about a seventh of B1 (Agent) ($0.079 against $0.542 per paper version). On its own, the citation checker scores an F1 of 0.879 on the HALLMARK reference benchmark.

How it works

1Read

GROBID parses the PDF into sentences, references, citations and tables. A planner turns them into small tasks: one per reference, citation, setting, method and claim.

2Check

Four checkers run in parallel. Citations: the reference exists and supports the sentence. Code and data: settings, metrics and methods match the repository. Theory: claims hold. Integrity: placeholders, impossible means, text against tables.

3Verify

The verifier looks for every quoted line again in its source. A finding whose quote is missing is dropped. One that rests on a record not being found goes to a person.

4Report

Each finding comes with its sentence or code lines and the quote. Verified flags, items for human review and checks that did not finish are listed apart. A person decides.

Limitations

  • Most labelled errors are planted. Only the 8 discrepancies reported by SciCoQA users are real, and they make 16 of the 163 labels.
  • All main runs use one model family (Gemini). B1 (Agent) has two runs, not three.
  • Method recall is low (F1 0.34). Most misses are code the model saw but did not report.
  • We have not checked the unlabelled flags on the original papers by hand, so we cannot yet say how many are real problems.
